\section{Analysis of Quantitative Results}

This section analyzes the quantitative performance trends observed in Table~\ref{tab:quantitative_comparison_merged}, focusing on how the proposed 
SFSAGE model compares against AGE and SAGE baselines across the Flowers and Animal Faces datasets at both 1024×1024 and 256×256 resolutions using 
FID and LPIPS metrics.

\subsection{102 Flowers}

\subsubsection{102 Flowers at 1024$\times$1024}

At high resolution (1024$\times$1024), SFSAGE demonstrates substantial improvements in distributional realism. The framework achieves an FID of 45.6, representing a 25.9\% reduction relative to SAGE (61.57) and a 36.0\% reduction relative to AGE (71.2). This significant decrease in FID indicates that SFSAGE generates images more closely aligned with the true distribution of flower images in the Inception feature space, reflecting improved statistical similarity to real data.

Critically, SFSAGE also achieves the highest LPIPS score of 0.45 compared to SAGE (0.24) and AGE (0.19), representing an 87.5\% improvement over SAGE and a 137\% improvement over AGE. This higher LPIPS reflects greater perceptual diversity in the generated samples, indicating that SFSAGE successfully explores a wider range of the latent-feature space rather than replicating limited reference images. The feature editing module enables variation across multiple semantic dimensions while maintaining category consistency. This increased diversity is essential in few-shot scenarios, where the risk of mode collapse and memorization is high.

However, higher LPIPS also implies a trade-off: generated images deviate more from their reference samples at the perceptual feature level. This means that while the outputs are diverse and distributionally realistic, they exhibit lower perceptual similarity to the original inputs in the VGG feature space. The framework prioritizes semantic exploration over strict fidelity to reference images.

\subsubsection{102 Flowers at 256$\times$256}

At lower resolution (256$\times$256), SFSAGE maintains its advantage in distributional realism with an FID of 145.6, outperforming SAGE (160.0) by 9.0\% and AGE (171.1) by 14.9\%. However, a notable divergence appears in the LPIPS metric: SFSAGE records 0.13, which is 55.2\% lower than SAGE (0.29) and 35.0\% lower than AGE (0.20). This indicates significantly reduced diversity at lower resolution compared to SAGE, though the gap with AGE is smaller.

This resolution-dependent behavior suggests that the feature editing module's effectiveness is tied to the dimensionality of the intermediate feature representations. At 256$\times$256, the feature tensors 
$\mathbf{F}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{512 \times 16 \times 16}$ contain substantially less spatial information than at 1024$\times$1024, where 
$\mathbf{F}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{512 \times 64 \times 64}$, constraining the module's capacity to introduce variation while preserving semantic coherence. 

The dramatically lower LPIPS compared to high resolution (0.13 vs. 0.45) suggests that SFSAGE adopts a conservative editing strategy at reduced resolution, prioritizing distributional alignment (FID) over perceptual diversity (LPIPS). The generated outputs likely remain closer to the class embedding with minimal attribute variation, reducing the risk of semantic drift but limiting the range of diverse samples.

\subsection{Animal Faces}

\subsubsection{Animal Faces HQ at 1024$\times$1024}

On the Animal Faces HQ dataset at high resolution (1024$\times$1024), SFSAGE achieves the lowest FID of 48.6, outperforming SAGE (60.7) by 19.9\% and AGE (73.3) by 33.7\%. This improvement reflects SFSAGE's enhanced capability to model the underlying distribution of animal facial images, as measured by the Fréchet distance between real and generated feature distributions in the Inception embedding space.

However, unlike the Flowers dataset, SFSAGE records a lower LPIPS score of 0.35 compared to SAGE (0.48) and AGE (0.39), representing a 27.1\% decrease relative to SAGE and a 10.3\% decrease relative to AGE. This reduced perceptual diversity at high resolution is notable and suggests dataset-dependent behavior in the feature editing module. The Animal Faces dataset may present different structural constraints in the latent-feature space compared to Flowers, where higher diversity (LPIPS 0.45) was achieved.

The lower LPIPS indicates that SFSAGE generates samples with less perceptual variation in the VGG feature space while still maintaining superior distributional alignment (lower FID). This suggests a more conservative editing strategy on this dataset, where the framework prioritizes maintaining closer perceptual similarity to reference inputs while improving overall distributional realism. The dual-level editing of both latent codes and feature tensors appears to operate differently across datasets, adapting to the complexity and variability inherent in each domain.

\subsubsection{Animal Faces HQ at 256$\times$256}

At 256$\times$256 resolution, SFSAGE continues to demonstrate superior distributional alignment with an FID of 46.3, outperforming SAGE (60.92) by 24.0\% and AGE (69.1) by 33.0\%. However, LPIPS drops to 0.33, which is 31.3\% lower than SAGE (0.48) and 10.8\% lower than AGE (0.37). This represents a reduction in perceptual diversity compared to both baselines at this resolution, though the gap is less pronounced than in the Flowers dataset at 256$\times$256.

Notably, SFSAGE maintains relatively consistent LPIPS across resolutions (0.35 at 1024$\times$1024 vs. 0.33 at 256$\times$256), exhibiting only a 5.7\% decrease. This stability contrasts sharply with the Flowers dataset, where LPIPS dropped from 0.45 to 0.13—a 71.1\% reduction. The more stable diversity metrics suggest that the Animal Faces dataset presents different structural properties in the latent-feature space that allow the feature editing module to maintain diversity even at lower spatial resolutions.

Despite the reduced diversity compared to baselines, SFSAGE achieves the strongest FID improvements at this resolution, indicating that the framework successfully balances distributional alignment with perceptual variation on Animal Faces. The feature editing module appears to adapt its strategy based on dataset characteristics, maintaining a moderate level of diversity while prioritizing distributional fidelity at lower resolutions.
