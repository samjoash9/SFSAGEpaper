\section{Qualitative Evaluation}

This section examines representative generated images to complement the quantitative findings. Each figure is presented together with a concise discussion of the observed differences across AGE, SAGE, and the proposed FeatureSAGE model. The outputs are grouped by dataset: 102 Flowers and Animal Faces.

On the other hand, the proposed model produces sharper petals than the input images and remains competitive despite minor inconsistencies. The level of contrast in the proposed model results in the sharpest flower among all the models.

The advantages of FeatureSAGE are further highlighted by Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_input2}. The sharpness in petal edges is better retained by the model compared to AGE and SAGE, which allows for a more photo-realistic and organic rendering. More varied outputs have also resulted from FeatureSAGE; for instance, in one generated image, the flower is presented with a slight tilt, reflecting strong attribute manipulation. The model maintains higher clarity, with balanced contrast and natural color distribution, while AGE looks slightly pale with reduced contrast in areas, which is somewhat settled by SAGE as it balances the contrast. Overall, the generated flower images produced by FeatureSAGE are the sharpest, most realistic, and visually consistent as compared to the other models being tested.

\label{fig:qualitative_animalfaces}

As observed in Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_animalfaces}, the outputs produced by all models manage to maintain the color and texture of the input data, implying that the basic characteristics of the reference image are preserved. Considering clarity and image properties, all the outputs can be considered similar, since there is minimal blurring effect and texture preservation on the fur of the input image. Consistency and overall properties, however, differ significantly: there is more diversity apparent in the outputs produced by AGE and SAGE, with slight differences in orientation between the two outcomes produced by each model. The more consistent results produced by the proposed FeatureSAGE model, on the contrary, demonstrate success toward maintaining integrity while providing semantic edits. Minor enhancements in realism are also observed, whereby the output from FeatureSAGE shows slightly more coherent shading and blending of the fur patterns. This indicates that although all these models demonstrate the ability to sustain basic attributes like color and texture, the FeatureSAGE model has the best consistency in output.

Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_input4} shows behavior similar to the previous animal-face input for FeatureSAGE, where it maintains consistency in terms of pose within the results, indicating that it does not exhibit great variability compared to AGE and SAGE, which produce more diverse orientations. At the same time, FeatureSAGE is better at maintaining consistency of shape and structure for the eyes, thus improving realism. The results from FeatureSAGE also show better clarity and contrast, improving image quality, particularly for fur texture and shading. On the other hand, AGE performs best in terms of diversity among all models, particularly for pose variation; however, this diversity may also decrease image clarity.
