\subsection{Visual Observations}

\subsubsection{Flowers}

Figures~\ref{fig:qualitative_comparison} and \ref{fig:qualitative_input2} compare generated flower images from AGE, SAGE, and FeatureSAGE. The visual analysis focuses on color preservation, edge sharpness, contrast, and semantic consistency. In Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_comparison}, FeatureSAGE retains the pink petal color of the input image and produces sharper petal boundaries than the baseline outputs. The generated image also shows stronger contrast, which contributes to a more defined flower structure. However, the model does not fully preserve the combined red and yellow colors of the pistil from the input image, indicating that fine internal flower details may still be difficult to reconstruct consistently.

Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_input2} further shows that FeatureSAGE preserves petal-edge clarity and produces visually coherent flower outputs. One generated output presents a slight tilt, demonstrating that the model can introduce appearance variation while retaining the flower category. Compared with AGE, which appears paler in some regions, and SAGE, which improves contrast but remains less sharp in the shown examples, FeatureSAGE produces clearer and more realistic flower structures in the qualitative comparisons.

\subsubsection{Animal Faces}

Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_animalfaces} compares AGE, SAGE, and FeatureSAGE on an Animal Faces input. All models preserve the basic color and fur texture of the input, but the outputs differ in structural consistency and local realism. AGE and SAGE show more visible variation in orientation between their two generated outputs. FeatureSAGE produces more consistent pose and structure while maintaining coherent fur shading and blending. This supports the quantitative observation that FeatureSAGE improves fidelity while preserving usable perceptual variation.

Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_input4} shows similar behavior for the second dog input. FeatureSAGE maintains more consistent eye shape and facial structure than the baselines in the displayed results. Its outputs also show clearer fur texture and stronger contrast. AGE produces greater pose diversity in this example, but the added variation is accompanied by reduced visual clarity. These observations are consistent with the LPIPS results, where FeatureSAGE remains close to the baselines in perceptual variation while achieving the lowest FID on the Animal Faces dataset.
