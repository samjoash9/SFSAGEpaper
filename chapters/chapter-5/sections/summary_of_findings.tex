\section{Summary of Findings}

This study developed and evaluated FeatureSAGE, a modification of the SAGE framework that integrates the StyleFeatureEditor inverter and Feature Editor into the few-shot attribute group editing pipeline. The findings are summarized according to the main contributions and evaluation objectives of the study.

\paragraph{1. Integration of the StyleFeatureEditor inverter.}
FeatureSAGE replaces the pSp-centered inversion stage of SAGE with the StyleFeatureEditor inverter, allowing the framework to represent input images using both $W^+$ latent codes and intermediate feature tensors. This modification produced lower FID scores than both baselines on the two evaluated datasets. At 1024~\(\times\)~1024 resolution, FeatureSAGE obtained an FID of 45.60 on 102 Flowers and 49.60 on Animal Faces.

\paragraph{2. Use of feature-level editing.}
The Feature Editor modifies intermediate feature representations during the decoder stage, supporting detail preservation while latent-space edits control semantic variation. Qualitative comparisons show sharper petal boundaries, clearer flower structure, and more coherent animal-face texture and shading in the FeatureSAGE outputs compared with the displayed AGE and SAGE outputs.

\paragraph{3. Quantitative improvement over AGE and SAGE.}
On the 102 Flowers dataset, FeatureSAGE improved FID by 15.8\% over SAGE and 25.9\% over AGE. On the Animal Faces dataset, FeatureSAGE improved FID by 18.6\% over SAGE and 20.2\% over AGE. These results show that the proposed inversion and feature-editing changes improved distributional alignment with real images under the one-shot setting.

\paragraph{4. Preservation of perceptual variation.}
FeatureSAGE maintained LPIPS values close to the baselines while achieving the best FID scores. On 102 Flowers, FeatureSAGE obtained an LPIPS of 0.4209, compared with 0.4528 for SAGE and 0.4108 for AGE. On Animal Faces, FeatureSAGE obtained an LPIPS of 0.4811, compared with 0.4867 for SAGE and 0.5033 for AGE. Thus, the improvement in FID did not remove perceptual variation among generated samples.
