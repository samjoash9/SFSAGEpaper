\section{Conclusion}

This study addressed the problem of improving few-shot image generation in the SAGE framework, particularly the limitation caused by relying on pSp-based inversion. The proposed FeatureSAGE framework integrates the StyleFeatureEditor inverter and Feature Editor so that images are represented not only by $W^+$ latent codes but also by intermediate feature tensors used during synthesis.

The results answer the research problem affirmatively within the scope of the evaluated datasets and metrics. Integrating the StyleFeatureEditor inverter and Feature Editor improved generated-image distribution alignment under the one-shot setting. FeatureSAGE achieved the lowest FID in all reported comparisons: 45.60 on 102 Flowers and 49.60 on Animal Faces. These correspond to FID improvements of 15.8\% and 18.6\% over SAGE, and 25.9\% and 20.2\% over AGE, respectively. At the same time, LPIPS remained comparable to the baselines, with 0.4209 on Flowers and 0.4811 on Animal Faces, showing that the fidelity gains did not eliminate perceptual variation.

The findings address the research questions as follows. First, the integration of the StyleFeatureEditor inverter and Feature Editor improved the inversion-and-editing pipeline as reflected by lower FID scores and sharper qualitative outputs. Second, the modifications influenced generated-image quality while maintaining comparable LPIPS values, indicating that consistency and diversity remained balanced in the reported one-shot setting. Third, FeatureSAGE retained the SAGE attribute group editing structure while adding feature-level editing, supporting reliable category-preserving edits in the displayed qualitative results. Fourth, compared with AGE and SAGE, FeatureSAGE achieved the strongest overall quantitative performance on FID across both datasets while remaining close to the baselines in LPIPS.

The study contributes four main findings: it integrates StyleFeatureEditor inversion into SAGE, adds feature-level editing through the Feature Editor, demonstrates consistent FID improvement over AGE and SAGE on both datasets, and shows through LPIPS and qualitative comparisons that perceptual variation and visual coherence are maintained. Therefore, FeatureSAGE provides a stronger few-shot attribute group editing framework for the tested Animal Faces and 102 Flowers settings, while its dependence on StyleGAN2 and its higher computational cost remain important limitations for future work.
