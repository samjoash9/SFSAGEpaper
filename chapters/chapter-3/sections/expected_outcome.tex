\section{Expected Outcome}

The study is expected to significantly enhance latent code retrieval from real-world images using the StyleFeatureEditing module incorporated in the SAGE framework, which in turn provides both improved reconstructability as well as editability without the common compromise between the two. Replacing the original encoder utilizing pSp, the FeatureSAGE framework is expected to achieve better downstream tasks such as data augmentation, especially in few-shot conditions with minimal data. Better inverting quality is expected to provide more accurate real-world image reconstructions at the same time maintaining the semantic properties important for successful editing. Moreover, the inclusion of the Feature Editor module is also expected to further improve the reconstructed images in terms of visual fidelity, retaining fine details, and maintaining identity consistency before as well as after the process of editing.

In order to evaluate FeatureSAGE, we shall measure its reconstruction ability on the following three benchmark datasets: Animal Faces, VGGFace2, and NABirds. This shall be measured using standard protocols such as Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) to measure image diversity and quality, Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) to measure perceptual closeness, and identity similarity ratings to measure preservation of subject identity. The usefulness of the framework in few-shot learning shall be measured in terms of the Naïve Augmentation Score (NAS), which measures if the synthetic images created using FeatureSAGE increase the accuracy of downstream classifiers. We shall also measure the disentanglement ability of the learned latent space using the Mutual Information Gap (MIG) value, which reflects the ability of generative factors to be represented independently of each other. We shall complete the benchmarking of FeatureSAGE by comparing it with current GAN inversion and few-shot image edit techniques to confirm its improvement in edit stability, preservation of identity, and general quality of the images.

Based on the objectives outlined in this study, the proposed FeatureSAGE framework is expected to achieve the following measurable outcomes:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Lower FID}, reflecting enhanced visual realism and distributional alignment of real and synthetic images.

    \item \textbf{Lower Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS)}, indicating higher perceptual closeness of original and reconstructed images.

    \item \textbf{Higher Naïve Augmentation Score (NAS)}, showing the strength of the synthetic images in enhanced real-world classification accuracy.

    \item \textbf{Higher Mutual Information Gap (MIG)}, indicating greater disentanglement of latent representations and more interpretable editing abilities.

    \item \textbf{Improved few-shot learning accuracy} with significant generalization from few data samples and stable attribute control.
    
\end{enumerate}


All of these results are collectively establish the strength of FeatureSAGE as a robust and universal instrument for few-shot image generation, manipulation, and data enhancement in resource-limited learning systems. The research also contribute to the development of the edit-based few-shot approach.
