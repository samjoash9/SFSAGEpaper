\section{Data Preprocessing}

\subsection{Data Sources}

This research uses publicly available datasets containing structured visual categories for few-shot image generation and attribute editing. The datasets are selected because they contain clearly grouped classes and visual variation that can be used to evaluate whether generated images preserve category-level structure while allowing appearance changes. Only a limited number of representative samples per class are used during few-shot evaluation to reproduce the data-scarce setting of the study. The datasets used are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{FUNIT Animal Faces Dataset}
    \begin{figure}[ht]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{images/funit_animal_faces_dataset.png}
        \caption{\textit{Sample images from the FUNIT Animal Faces dataset. Source: \citep{liu2019few}}}
        \label{fig:FUNIT_animal_faces}
    \end{figure}

    Figure~\ref{fig:FUNIT_animal_faces} shows representative samples from the FUNIT Animal Faces dataset. The dataset is composed of 117,484 animal face images at 512~\(\times\)~512 resolution and is organized into 149 classes, where each class corresponds to one animal species. Each domain includes a diverse range of species and breeds, ensuring variation in appearance while maintaining consistent facial alignment to support generative model training. The images were curated from openly available sources and standardized in resolution and alignment, with the eyes centrally positioned to provide consistency for learning. A subset of the images is reserved as a test set, with the remainder used for training. The diversity and quality of the FUNIT Animal Faces dataset make it a strong benchmark for generative and attribute-editing models in few-shot settings \citep{liu2019few}.

    \item \textbf{102 Flowers Dataset}
    \begin{figure}[ht]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{images/flowers_dataset.png}
        \caption{\textit{Sample images from the 102 Flowers dataset. Source: \citep{Nilsback08}}}
        \label{fig:102Flower}
    \end{figure}

    Figure~\ref{fig:102Flower} shows sample images from the 102 Flowers dataset, which comprises 102 flower classes found in the United Kingdom. Each class contains between 40 and 258 images. The dataset includes variation in flower size, pose, and lighting conditions, and it contains both large intra-class differences and visually similar categories. These characteristics make the dataset suitable for evaluating fine-grained category preservation and appearance variation. The raw images have varying dimensions; for example, one raw image from the dataset measures 500~\(\times\)~667 pixels \citep{Nilsback08}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Data Preprocessing Steps}

The same preprocessing pipeline is applied across the baseline SAGE and proposed FeatureSAGE configurations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Resizing and normalization:} Images are resized according to the StyleGAN2 configuration used in the experiment and are normalized to the range \([-1,1]\) before inversion or synthesis. The quantitative results reported in Chapter~4 are evaluated at 1024~\(\times\)~1024 resolution.

    \item \textbf{Filtering:} Extremely low-quality or mislabeled images are removed before training and evaluation. For the FUNIT Animal Faces dataset, MTCNN is used to crop and horizontally align animal faces so that the eyes are leveled.

    \item \textbf{Train-test splitting:} The datasets are partitioned into seen categories for model fitting and unseen categories for few-shot testing:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Animal Faces:} Out of the total 149 categories, 119 are used as seen classes for training, while the remaining 30 serve as unseen classes for evaluation.
      \item \textbf{Flowers:} Out of the total 102 categories, 85 are assigned to the seen training split and 17 are reserved for the unseen test split.
    \end{itemize}
    
    \item \textbf{Augmentation:} No augmentation is used beyond centering and flipping, since the study focuses on evaluating the same domains under controlled few-shot conditions.
\end{itemize}
