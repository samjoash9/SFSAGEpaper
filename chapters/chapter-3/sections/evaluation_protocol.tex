\section{Evaluation Protocol}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

The models are evaluated using quantitative metrics for generated-image distribution alignment and perceptual variation under a one-shot test-time setup. The same implementations and evaluation settings are used for AGE, SAGE, and FeatureSAGE.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Fr\'echet Inception Distance (FID)}

    Fr\'echet Inception Distance (FID) is used to evaluate how closely the distribution of generated images matches the distribution of real images \citep{heusel2017gans, wang2023attribute}. Instead of comparing images directly at the pixel level, FID computes statistics from a high-level feature space extracted using a pretrained Inception-v3 network. The metric models the real and generated image features as multivariate Gaussian distributions and computes the distance between them. Equation~\ref{eq:fid} defines the FID score:

    \begin{equation}
        \mathrm{FID} =
        \|\mu_r-\mu_g\|_2^2+
        \mathrm{Tr}\left(\Sigma_r+\Sigma_g-2(\Sigma_r\Sigma_g)^{1/2}\right).
        \label{eq:fid}
    \end{equation}
        
    In Equation~\ref{eq:fid}, $\mu_r$ and $\Sigma_r$ are the mean and covariance of Inception features from real images; $\mu_g$ and $\Sigma_g$ are the mean and covariance of Inception features from generated images; $\|\mu_r-\mu_g\|_2^2$ is the squared Euclidean distance between feature means; $\mathrm{Tr}(\cdot)$ is the trace operator; and $(\Sigma_r\Sigma_g)^{1/2}$ is the matrix square root of the covariance product. The theoretical basis of FID is the Fr\'echet, or 2-Wasserstein, distance between two Gaussian feature distributions \citep{heusel2017gans}. Lower FID values indicate stronger distributional alignment between generated and real images.

    \item \textbf{Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS)}
    
    Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) compares images using deep feature activations from a pretrained neural network, such as VGG or AlexNet, rather than relying only on pixel-level differences \citep{zhang2018unreasonable}. This makes LPIPS useful for evaluating perceptual similarity in reconstruction tasks and perceptual diversity when measured among generated outputs. Equation~\ref{eq:lpips} defines the LPIPS score between images $x$ and $x'$:

    \begin{equation}
    \mathrm{LPIPS}(x,x') =
    \sum_l \frac{1}{H_l W_l}
    \sum_{h=1}^{H_l}\sum_{w=1}^{W_l}
    \left\| \omega_l \odot
    \left(\hat{y}_{hw}^l(x)-\hat{y}_{hw}^l(x')\right)
    \right\|_2^2.
    \label{eq:lpips}
    \end{equation}

    In Equation~\ref{eq:lpips}, $\hat{y}_{hw}^l(x)$ is the unit-normalized feature vector of image $x$ at layer $l$ and spatial position $(h,w)$, $\omega_l$ is a learned channel-weight vector, and $H_l$ and $W_l$ are the height and width of the feature map at layer $l$. For reconstruction similarity, lower LPIPS values indicate closer perceptual similarity. In the results table, LPIPS is reported as a perceptual-diversity measure among generated outputs, so higher values indicate greater perceptual variation when image fidelity is maintained.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Baselines for Comparison}

To contextualize the performance of the proposed few-shot image generation model, FeatureSAGE is compared against two editing-based few-shot image generation baselines:

\begin{itemize}    
    \item \textbf{AGE} \citep{AGE}: Leverages latent-space attribute editing on pretrained GANs to produce novel-class images without retraining by disentangling class-relevant and category-irrelevant features.
    
    \item \textbf{SAGE} \citep{ding2023stableattributegroupediting}: Builds on AGE by aggregating support images to create stable latent embeddings and injecting class-consistent diversity through attribute mixing.
\end{itemize}
